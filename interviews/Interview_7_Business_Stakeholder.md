Interview 7 — Business Stakeholder
Topic: Commercial value of external banking connectivity, client onboarding, FX flow via Franx.
Setting: Franx office

Q1: What is the biggest barrier for new clients who want to start trading via Franx today?


The main barrier is the funding step. Before a client can trade, they need to transfer money to their Franx account first. 
That takes time and effort. A client who wants to trade today has to plan ahead, move funds, wait for the transfer to arrive, and only then can they execute. 
For a business that wants to act quickly on a currency opportunity, that delay is a real problem.

Q2: How would it change things if a client could initiate a payment directly from their ABN AMRO EUR account via Franx?


It would remove that barrier entirely. The client does not need to pre-fund anything. 
They see a rate they like, they click trade, and we initiate the payment from their ABN AMRO account in the background. 
The EUR arrives at Franx, we do the FX conversion, and we send the target currency to where it needs to go. 
The whole process becomes seamless from the client's perspective.

Q3: The FX conversion still happens at Franx even if the payment comes from an external ABN AMRO account, is that important?


Yes, that is a key point. We are not bypassing Franx for the actual trade. The payment initiation just moves the funding step outside of Franx. 
The rate, the conversion, the settlement, all of that still happens at Franx. So we keep the margin and the relationship. 
We are just making it easier for the client to get money into the flow.

Q4: Which client segment would benefit most from this?


ABN AMRO business clients are the obvious target. They already have a relationship with ABN AMRO and all of them have EUR accounts there. 
If they can connect that account directly to Franx without opening a new account or going through a separate onboarding process, the barrier to get started is much lower. 
Medium-sized businesses that do regular international payments are probably the best fit, 
they have enough volume to care about efficiency but are not so large that they have a dedicated treasury team handling everything manually.

Q5: Is there a risk that clients feel uncomfortable with Franx accessing their ABN AMRO account?


That is something we need to manage carefully. The client needs to understand exactly what Franx can and cannot do. 
We can initiate a payment that the client has approved, nothing more. We cannot move money without their explicit approval for each transaction. 
The bank connectivity agreement and the Access Online authorisation step make this clear. But we do need to explain it well in the onboarding flow. 

Q6: How does this affect Franx's position in the market?


It makes Franx more relevant for a larger group of clients. With direct account connectivity, Franx becomes a much easier platform to start with. 
It also strengthens the ABN AMRO relationship because we are making better use of the infrastructure that is already there. 
It is a win for the client, a win for Franx, and a win for ABN AMRO.
